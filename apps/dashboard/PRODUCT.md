# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Orvel Dashboard is used by beauty-business owners, receptionists, and solo professionals who manage appointments, clients, services, availability, and booking links during the workday.

They are often between clients, answering WhatsApp, checking today’s agenda, or trying to recover empty slots. They need fast confidence: what is happening today, what needs attention, and what action should happen next.

## Product Purpose

The dashboard is the operational control surface for Orvel. It should help a business owner turn a public booking link, working hours, client records, and appointments into a clear daily workflow.

Success means the user can understand their day in seconds, create or update records without friction, and trust that Orvel is protecting availability, income, and client communication.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, protective.

The product should feel like a premium command center, not a decorative landing page. It can share Orvel’s dark/violet identity, but the interface must prioritize task clarity, legibility, state feedback, and consistent component behavior.

## Anti-references

- Decorative SaaS dashboards where neon, blur, or oversized empty cards compete with the task.
- Generic admin panels that feel cold, gray, and disconnected from beauty-business reality.
- Modal-heavy workflows that interrupt simple actions without need.
- Empty states that only say “nothing here” instead of teaching the next action.
- Inconsistent forms where buttons, labels, focus rings, or validation states change screen to screen.

## Design Principles

1. Task clarity beats visual drama. Use color, motion, and hierarchy to explain state and next action, not to decorate.
2. Keep the dark command-center identity restrained. Violet marks selection, primary actions, and active states; semantic colors communicate status.
3. Empty states must be operational. When there are no appointments or clients, the UI should suggest the next useful action.
4. Forms must feel safe and fast. Inputs, labels, helper text, errors, and buttons should use the same vocabulary everywhere.
5. Density should serve the workday. Show enough information to decide quickly, but avoid making the owner parse noise between appointments.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for all text and controls. Preserve visible focus states, keyboard-friendly dialogs, reduced-motion alternatives, clear Spanish labels, and readable touch targets for users working from tablets or smaller laptops in a salon environment.
