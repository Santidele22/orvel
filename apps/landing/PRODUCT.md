# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Orvel speaks to beauty-business owners and operators in Argentina: peluquerías, barberías, nail salons, spas, massage studios, tattoo studios, brow/lash studios, and solo professionals who manage bookings through WhatsApp, notes, and manual reminders.

They are good at their craft but their day gets fragmented by scheduling, cancellations, reschedules, confirmations, and missed messages. They usually review the product between appointments, after hours, or while trying to regain control of a busy agenda.

## Product Purpose

Orvel turns booking chaos into a controlled, professional, always-on agenda. The landing must make the visitor feel that more demand does not have to mean more stress: clients can reserve, the business can protect income, and the owner can understand what happens next without living inside WhatsApp.

Success means the visitor believes Orvel is not “another calendar”; it is an operational layer that fills the agenda, reduces manual coordination, and gives the business owner time back.

## Brand Personality

Controlled, sharp, protective.

The brand should feel like a calm command center for a business that is usually emotionally and operationally overloaded. It can use tension in the copy and visuals, but the product should always feel composed, reliable, and premium.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI SaaS landing pages with purple mesh gradients, glass cards everywhere, and interchangeable feature grids.
- Cute salon/spa templates with pastel wellness clichés, stock-beauty softness, or decorative florals.
- Corporate enterprise dashboards that feel cold, bureaucratic, or built for managers instead of working salon owners.
- Busy marketplace-style booking pages that make the product feel like more work.
- “Another agenda/calendar” positioning that underplays revenue protection and operational control.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with the chaos-to-control transformation. Every fold should make the visitor feel the before/after: less WhatsApp pressure, more booked time, clearer operations.
2. Keep the dark command-center identity. The current black surface, violet control accent, and selective red chaos accents are strategic, not decorative.
3. Use tension sparingly. Red belongs to the problem and chaos; violet belongs to control, product action, and relief.
4. Make the product feel premium but usable. Strong typography, measured spacing, and clear hierarchy matter more than decorative effects.
5. Show the business reality. Beauty-category imagery and product screenshots should support the story, but never turn the page into a generic salon template.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast for body copy and controls. Preserve strong focus states, readable Spanish copy, reduced-motion alternatives, and mobile-first readability for owners checking the page from a phone between appointments.
